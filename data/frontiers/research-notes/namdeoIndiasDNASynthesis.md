### 2 Policy and regulatory landscape










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
two existing sets of laws and regulations provide frameworks under which DNA synthesis activities and their potential misuse could be considered by relevant authorities</span>
([2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=2&annotation=YMX9GE2Z))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
first one is the Environment Protection Law of 1986 and the associated 1989 regulation titled ‘Rules for the manufacture, use/ import/export and storage of hazardous microorganisms/genetically  engineered organisms or cells’</span>
([2](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=2&annotation=ZDKLNCX9))

<span style="color:

<span style="color:

<span style="color:

<span style="color:

<span style="color:

1.2.





<span style="color:

<span style="color:

### 3 Industry landscape










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
United States leads with 111 companies offering custom synthesis services, followed by China with 77. India ranks third, with 31 in-country custom nucleic acid companies</span>
([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=D6U4AIVJ))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
India accounts for approximately 9% of the global share of custom nucleic acid companies, ranking third worldwide, based on the total number of such companies identified across 74 countries, and first within Central and Southern Asia</span>
([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=6BKP73JF))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
59 companies associated with nucleic acid services were identified in India. Of these 59 companies, only 31 conduct custom in-country DNA synthesis, while 21 operate as third-party vendors</span>
([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=KJLXHR5W))





India Companies:

59 total nucleic acid companies

- 31 custom DNA synthesis
- 21 third-party vendors
- 7 related services (like sequencing)





<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
When it comes to screening practices, only a few companies have oligo order forms on their websites, which could be used to collect customer information with the possibility of conducting customer screening</span>
([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=SSBDMAMY))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
only the name of the gene is required</span>
([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=ECWQE4YN))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Indian companies are not members of the International Gene Synthesis Consortium (IGSC) and do not follow its protocols at the time of writing this article</span>
([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=Y35V5MCJ))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">

([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=QTLR952R))










<span style="color:

([3](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=3&annotation=6NS4DADT))

### 4 Policy recommendations and way forward










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
in the absence of a national biosecurity strategy, a specific DNA synthesis screening framework, and a defined “sequences of concern” list, customer and sequence screening for biosecurity risks is not mandatory for companies based in India selling at a national level</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=AXKK6BFI))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
significant number of companies conduct in-house gene and oligo synthesis in India, and several more are part of the distribution networks of largely U.S.-based companies</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=UXMGGC22))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
substantial effort would require close engagement with industry partners such as the Association of Biotechnology Led Enterprises (ABLE), the domestic biotech industry body</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=TK5JTHY3))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
open-source, and globally available tools such as IBBIS’s Common Mechanism</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=7AC79PA8))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
At present, DNA synthesis screening represents one of the most practical and effective safeguards available to reduce the potential misuse of synthetic DNA</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=4JH3FDU6))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
recent work by Microsoft researchers and collaborators, including IBBIS, demonstrated that generative protein design tools can produce substantially modified protein sequences that may evade conventional sequence-based screening approaches, prompting subsequent improvements to existing screening tools</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=YK3DT46S))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Common Mechanism has similarly continued to evolve and is designed to provide greater resilience against screening-evasion attempts, including AI-generated sequences</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=P4C9IGB9))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
concrete near-term steps, DBT should assume a central coordinating role, in consultation with DGFT and ICMR, and initiate the development of a national “sequences of concern” framework</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=BM8MY9SD))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
existing international frameworks such as the IGSC Harmonized Screening Protocol, along with a reporting mechanism for flagged orders</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=YJSJEGL5))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) and DBT should establish a certification scheme for domestic synthesis providers that adopt sequence and customer screening</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=72JESCJL))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
incentive-based, with public research funding and government procurement progressively favoring synthesis providers that demonstrate adherence to recognized customer and sequence screening practices</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=PNDEN35D))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
proportion of domestic synthesis providers conducting both sequence and customer screening, participation in a national certification scheme, the number of relevant IBSCs receiving dedicated biosecurity training, and the establishment and use of mechanisms for reporting and escalating potentially concerning orders</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=GLVMAXHS))





Metrics to track during transformation / Indicators.





<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
If voluntary adoption and market incentives fail to achieve sufficient screening coverage, India should consider progressively stronger measures, including linking compliance</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=W2BC68G8))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
U.S. framework leverages federal funding and procurement mechanisms to promote compliance</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=4&annotation=29GA8IZP))





Incentive-based.





<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
2024 U.S. Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening established expectations for sequence-of-concern screening, customer legitimacy assessment, reporting of potentially illegitimate orders, record retention, and procurement from providers adhering to the framework</span>
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=5&annotation=6TFW56H6))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Executive Order 14,292, issued in May 2025, directed the Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP) to revise or replace both the 2024 Framework for Nucleic Acid Synthesis Screening and the 2024 U.S. Government Policy for Oversight of Dual Use Research of Concern and Pathogens with Enhanced Pandemic Potential</span>
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=5&annotation=JCU4FWPV))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
the adoption of nucleic acid synthesis screening should be understood as a continuum rather than as a binary distinction between countries that screen and those that do not</span>
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/VBKMYR9B?page=5&annotation=EBSE2ZHD))
