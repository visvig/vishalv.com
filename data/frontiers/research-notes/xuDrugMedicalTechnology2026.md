abstract: "Whether a cell or gene therapy (CGT) is regulated as a drug or as a medical technology determines who approves it, on what evidence, and at what cost. China answers this question with a dual-track system. On May 1, 2026, China’s Regulation on the Clinical Research and Translational Application Management of New Biomedical Technologies (State Council Order No. 818) took effect, giving that system its most authoritative legislative form to date, supplemented by three implementing instruments issued in April 2026. The first track regulates CGT as a biologic under the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA). The second regulates it as a medical technology under the National Health Commission (NHC) and contains two subtypes that the law now separates explicitly: non-commercial investigator-initiated research (Article 20), in which participants cannot be charged, and fee-charging translational application (Article 34). Building on earlier accounts of China’s system, and drawing on the Regulation and its implementing documents, this Perspective clarifies the dual-track architecture and sets it beside the regenerative-medicine frameworks of Japan, Korea, the European Union, and the United States. Compared this way, China’s drug track is conventional by international standards. Its medical-technology track is the distinctive part: a statutory line between non-commercial research and fee-charging application (Articles 20 and 34); a risk-tiered period that confines a newly approved technology to its originating institutions for five, three, or one year; and a published, rule-based classification mechanism under Article 55, operated through a positive list with sponsor self-classification, NHC advisory support, and joint NHC–NMPA deliberation when the list is revised. For readers abroad, the practical point is that the track on which a Chinese study sits is part of how its evidence should be read."

### The dual-track architecture










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
drug track means regulation of a CGT as a biologic under the Drug Administration Law: NMPA review, IND then BLA, national marketing authorization</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=4&annotation=U885ZM7Q))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
medical-technology track means regulation of the same class of intervention as a clinical technique under the NHC, without marketing authorization</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=4&annotation=BXKLKP6I))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Clinical research is the non-commercial activity governed by Articles 15–25: it requires prior academic and ethics review, is filed with the NHC within five working days of that review, may be conducted only at tertiary-A hospitals, and may not charge participants for anything related to the research</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=4&annotation=VZ67KFKQ))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Translational application is the downstream fee-charging activity governed by Articles 29–34: it requires a national translational-application approval from the NHC before any patient is treated or billed</span>
([4](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=4&annotation=NB4Z2LB2))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">

### Box 1. How the drug/technology boundary is drawn under Article 55










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
NHC issues a positive-list Filing Guidance Catalog against which a sponsor self-classifies before starting work: a sponsor that self-classifies as a technology files under the Catalog’s standardized technology name and filing requirements, and one that self-classifies as a drug or device proceeds under drug or device law</span>
([7](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=7&annotation=UURNC5N6))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
The Catalog is adjusted in both directions. A technology is removed directly if it is  prohibited by law, raises major ethical problems, shows major safety or efficacy problems, or generates uncontrollable risk or serious adverse social impact — whereupon ongoing filed studies must be terminated with participants’ interests protected</span>
([7](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=7&annotation=NPYVTEQT))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
only formal challenge routes are downstream and sponsor-facing: a sponsor whose translational application is recommended for refusal may object within five working days, triggering expert re-deliberation, and a refusal decision carries rights of administrative reconsideration and litigation</span>
([8](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=8&annotation=25IMINUA))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Approval is limited to technologies that are highly individualized and lack a same-mechanism marketed or pivotal-trial drug, or to rare-disease technologies that likewise lack an equivalent drug</span>
([8](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=8&annotation=F5CS8I4B))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
fully patient-specific neoantigen-directed cellular products, autologous tissue-engineered constructs that resist standardized manufacture, and gene therapies for ultra-rare inherited disorders</span>
([8](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=8&annotation=8HDCW7N5))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
the technology is released to the wider health system only after the originating centers have accumulated postapproval experience, which places the burden of confirmation on the implementing institution rather than, as under a conditional marketing approval, on the product sponsor</span>
([8](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=8&annotation=RIUX6C4D))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Every treated case is reported to the provincial health authority through the national information system, and serious adverse reactions immediately</span>
([9](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=9&annotation=DMEB8CE2))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Nothing in the Regulation or its implementing rules requires NMPA good-manufacturing-practice certification or the pharmaceutical quality system that attends a biologic; quality is assured technique by technique and institution by institution, with quality-control standards assessed in the approval dossier</span>
([9](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=9&annotation=22EZ5XYL))

### The two tracks in international perspective










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
take the two tracks in turn, reading each against Japan, Korea, the European Union, and the United States</span>
([10](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=10&annotation=AASW6RSX))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Japan pursues early availability with deferred proof: a product may reach patients on confirmed safety and presumed efficacy, the burden discharged — or the approval withdrawn — during a time-limited post-marketing period</span>
([10](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=10&annotation=FTBJWLI4))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Korea pursues access expansion, its 2024 amendment opening to all indications a route once reserved for rare and life-threatening conditions</span>
([10](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=10&annotation=BTNHCWL6))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
European Union pursues single-market integrity: centralized authorization is the rule and the hospital exemption a bounded exception to it, which is why it is confined to non-routine, custommade, single-Member-State provision</span>
([10](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=10&annotation=WSCD75NF))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
United States is product-centered, with individual-patient rather than institutional exceptions: expanded access permits use of an unapproved investigational product outside a trial, on FDA authorization and institutional review board review, with charging limited to cost recovery</span>
([10](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=10&annotation=QK84KK4Z))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
China is research-driven and institutionally contained: approval is earned by multicenter research evidence rather than by a sponsor&#39;s confirmatory commitment, and diffusion is throttled at the institution rather than the product</span>
([10](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=10&annotation=LEI4E29D))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
who bears the risk of incomplete evidence — the sponsor, the treating institution, or the individual patient and physician</span>
([10](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=10&annotation=Q6BY8NUM))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Japan and Korea each add a step China does not take: a dedicated, expedited route for the product itself</span>
([11](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=11&annotation=FGBBII5M))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
China’s drug track carries no CGT-specific acceleration statute of this kind. Instead, four statutory accelerated review and approval procedures—breakthrough therapy designation, conditional approval, priority review and approval, and special approval procedure</span>
([11](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=11&annotation=B62UI2RA))

### Implications for developers, clinicians, and regulators










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
Three elements of the Chinese model are relevant for international readers: pre-submission boundary classification, positive-list filing, and a statutory separation between research and fee-charging application</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=4LW7HE5Y))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
For developers, they affect the choice between an NMPA product pathway and an NHC medical-technology pathway</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=QSIR5BKM))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
For clinicians, they help determine how much weight to give to evidence generated in Chinese investigator-initiated trials (IITs) or translational applications</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=FVBFSH2L))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
For regulators, they illustrate one way to manage the risk that fee-charging cell therapies become an alternative route to market</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=ANTV6NL8))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
The first is the inter-agency boundary mechanism</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=FVA8QSWT))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
China’s answer is rule-based rather than adjudicative: under Article 55 a sponsor self-classifies against a published positive list before committing to a track, with an NHC-designated body available to advise and with joint NHCNMPA deliberation reserved for revising the list</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=M3PRSCV5))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
The second is the positive-list filing catalog, which is most relevant to developers planning an early CGT program</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=N73G2KLT))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
For fields moving as fast as gene editing, cell engineering, and microbiota therapeutics, a maintained list trades some flexibility for a great deal of predictability</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=2ILBYDMR))










<span style="color:
#FFF176;">
The third is the statutory separation of non-commercial research (Article 20) from fee-charging application</span>
([13](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/E346IJHQ?page=13&annotation=JCA4NRQ6))










<span style="color:
