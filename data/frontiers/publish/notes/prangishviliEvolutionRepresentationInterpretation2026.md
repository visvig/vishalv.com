### 3. The Genetic Code and the Differentiation of Representation and Interpretation










> mechanism capable of reliably connecting two distinct representational domains: the nucleotide sequences of nucleic acids and the amino acid sequences of proteins
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=NFMI6DLC))










> systematic relationship between codons and amino acids
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=39KVKR5R))





Codon: triplet code






> coordinated action of transfer RNAs, aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, and the translational machinery, nucleotide sequences came to specify amino acid sequences
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=7HK824Q7))










> Representation and interpretation thus became partially independent components
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=6WDBRZ63))










> emergence of translation created a new level of representational autonomy
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=7TBDLHRD))










> epistemic cut: the distinction between symbolic description and physical dynamics
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=V4397WTP))










> Proteins produced through translation became essential for maintaining and reproducing the very machinery required for their own synthesis
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=7SYW6A44))










> Aminoacyl-tRNA synthetases, ribosomal proteins, metabolic enzymes, and nucleotide-synthesizing pathways collectively formed a network in which representations, interpretations, and functional activities became mutually dependent
([5](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=5&annotation=FR7ADP8E))










> reciprocal dependency created one of the first autonomous representational architectures in evolution
([6](zotero://open-pdf/library/items/4J228UNM?page=6&annotation=AYIIRR8M))










### 4. Language and the Social Distribution of Representation and Interpretation










> As nervous systems evolved, organisms acquired the capacity to construct increasingly sophisticated internal representations of their environments through patterns of neural activity

> neural representations remained closely tied to the individual organism and its sensorimotor experience

> emergence of language transformed this condition by enabling representations to be externalized, shared, and preserved beyond the boundaries of individual minds
